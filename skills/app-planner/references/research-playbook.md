# Research playbook

Budget: max 8 searches per session. Spend them in this order and stop when you have enough.

## Search order
1. **Category demand** — `"<category> app" trends <current year>`; Google Trends interest for the core keyword (rising / flat / falling).
2. **Play Store competitors** — search `site:play.google.com <keyword>`; fetch the top 3–5 listings: installs band, rating, last updated, price model, in-app purchase range.
3. **Complaints = gaps** — read 1–3★ reviews of the top 2 competitors (fetch the listing or a review aggregator). Note repeated complaints only.
4. **Revenue signal** — public reports from Appfigures, Sensor Tower, data.ai blogs, RevenueCat "State of Subscription Apps", or founder posts (Indie Hackers, X, Reddit). Always label as estimate with source and date.
5. **Regional angle** (if SG/SEA) — local competitors, local payment habits, language.

## Reading signals
| Signal | Good sign | Warning sign |
|---|---|---|
| Top apps' last update | Within 3 months | Abandoned → opportunity *or* dead niche |
| Ratings | Leaders at 3.5–4.2★ with clear complaints | Leaders at 4.7★+ with no pattern in complaints |
| Installs | Several apps at 100K–1M | One giant at 100M+ and nothing in between |
| Monetization | Competitors charge and still get good reviews | Everything free, ad-only, low ratings |
| Trend | Rising for 12+ months | Spike from a single viral moment |

## Idea generation (mode B)
Generate 5 ideas, each one line + why now. Draw from:
- Rising Google Trends terms with weak apps
- Top-grossing Play categories with poorly rated leaders
- AI-enabled upgrades of old app types (scanner, journal, planner, tutor, translator)
- The user's own domains and automation skills (unfair advantages)
- Local SG/SEA needs underserved by global apps
Present as a table: idea | target user | money model | build size (S/M/L) | why now. User picks one.

## Revenue estimate rules
- Never state a revenue number without a source.
- If only download estimates exist: show a range using clearly stated assumptions (e.g. "if 2% convert at $3/month…") and label it **assumption-based**.
- Prefer ranges over single numbers.
